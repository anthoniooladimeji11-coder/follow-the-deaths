Method

How the index is built, and what the labels mean.

The index in one sentence

The share of total funding that would have to move between pathogens for each one's funding share to equal its share of the burden. 0% is perfect alignment; it works for any list of pathogens and any burden measure.

GMI = ½ Σ |funding share − burden share| (total-variation distance)

Labels

  • Neglected: under-funded, and a below-median share of late-stage projects.
  • Pipeline OK: under-funded, but well stocked with late-stage drugs.
  • Well-resourced: over-funded, with a strong pipeline.
  • Low translation: over-funded, but few late-stage drugs.
  • WHO priority: tier on the WHO Bacterial Priority Pathogens List 2024, shown with the resistance type it applies to (e.g. carbapenem-resistant). Matched from the name.
  • Resistance: share of isolates in the extensively drug-resistant group (latent class analysis), Vivli AMR Register. ↑ rising, → flat, 2019–2024.
  • Genus artefact: funding recorded for a whole genus that includes other species or vaccine work, so the gap is not interpretable.

6 leading pathogens dataset

Funding and pipeline: Global AMR R&D Hub (18,853 projects, USD 18.9B; USD 4.17B names one of the six). Burden: GRAM 2019, The Lancet 2022. Resistance and 2030: 17.5 million Vivli AMR Register lab results (ATLAS, KEYSTONE, SIDERO-WT); Bayesian projection to 2030. Funding is tagged by genus. Index range across 6 funding/burden versions: 15.7–25.3%; 2030 80% range: 23.0–32.0%.

Limits

The index compares shares, so it is only as good as the funding attribution and burden estimates you enter. It measures alignment with burden, not scientific opportunity; treat it as an audit, not an allocation formula.
